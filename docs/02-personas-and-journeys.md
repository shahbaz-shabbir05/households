# 02 — Personas and User Journeys

Personas are drawn from the household the brief describes (a Pakistani family
household, generalisable internationally). They exist to settle design arguments,
not for decoration — each one has a *veto* attached.

## Personas

### 1. Ayesha — the household operator (primary, ~70% of usage)
- 38, manages the home day to day. Phone-first, often one-handed, often in the
  kitchen or the car.
- Currently holds everything in her head plus three WhatsApp chats.
- **Wants:** to stop being the single point of failure. To see today at a glance.
- **Pain:** entering data is a chore on top of the chore.
- **Veto:** *if any capture flow takes more than ~15 seconds or more than 4 taps,
  it is wrong.* She will not use it.

### 2. Bilal — the finance-and-bills parent (secondary, ~20%)
- 42, works full time, checks the app 2–3× a week, usually in the evening.
- **Wants:** what's due, what's overdue, what did we spend this month, was the
  electricity bill actually paid.
- **Pain:** finding out about a bill after it's late.
- **Veto:** *if the app cannot tell him what is due in the next 7 days on the
  first screen, it has failed its main job.*

### 3. Zara — the teenager (light, ~5%)
- 15. Sees her own tasks, school events, her own schedule.
- **Must not see:** household finances, documents, other members' medical data.
- **Veto:** *if she can see a CNIC scan or the monthly expense total, the
  authorization model is broken.* This is a security test case, not a preference.

### 4. Dada/Dadi — the elder member (light, occasional)
- 70. Large text, high contrast, minimal navigation. Mostly a *recipient*:
  their medicines and appointments are managed by Ayesha.
- **Veto:** *if the medicine screen requires more than reading and one tap to
  confirm a dose, it is unusable for them.*

### 5. Sana — the domestic helper (restricted)
- Assigned chores. Sees a task list and nothing else. Often has no login at all —
  Ayesha marks her tasks done.
- **Veto:** *a member must be assignable without having an account.* This single
  requirement is why `members` and `users` are separate tables.

### 6. Admin-Ayesha (same person, different hat)
- Sets up the household, invites members, sets roles, defines categories.
- Used rarely — so setup must be forgiving and reversible, not a wizard you must
  complete perfectly on day one.

## Core journeys

Each journey states the target interaction cost. These are design constraints.

### J1 — First run (target: usable household in < 3 minutes)
1. Sign up (email, password, name) → email verification sent, **but not blocking**.
2. Create household: name, country → currency + timezone defaulted from it, both editable.
3. "Who lives here?" — add members with name + relationship only. Everything else
   (DOB, photo, phone) is optional and can be filled later.
4. Land on the dashboard, which is honest about being empty: it shows *empty
   states that are calls to action* ("Add your first bill — we'll remind you
   before it's due"), not zeros.

**Design rule:** verification does not block usage; it blocks only invites and
password-sensitive actions. A blocking verification screen loses users at the
exact moment they are most willing to try.

### J2 — Morning check (target: < 30s, the most frequent journey)
Ayesha opens the app → Dashboard `Today`:
- Overdue items first, in red, with count.
- Then due today: tasks, bills, medicines, appointments, events.
- Each row is actionable inline: tick a task, mark a bill paid, snooze.
- No navigation needed to *act*; navigation is only for *browsing*.

### J3 — Quick capture (target: < 15s, 4 taps)
Floating "+" → 9 quick actions → sheet with **only the required fields**:
- Expense: amount, category (recent-first), date (defaults today). Done.
- Grocery: item name (autocomplete from history). Done.
- Task: title, assignee (defaults self), due (chips: today/tomorrow/this week).
Advanced fields live behind "More details", never in the primary path.

### J4 — Shopping run (the module-composition showcase)
1. Inventory drops below minimum → dashboard suggests "Milk is low — add to list?"
2. Ayesha opens the shopping list, grouped by category (aisle order), ticks items
   as she shops.
3. At checkout, "Done shopping" → enter total + payment method →
   **one expense is created**, inventory quantities are **incremented**, items
   move to shopping history, and the recurring items re-queue on their cadence.

This one flow is the difference between "a system" and "a collection of CRUD
screens" (§46). If nothing else works, this must.

### J5 — Bill lifecycle
Bill created (or auto-generated by recurrence) → reminder fires N days before →
Bilal marks paid, entering amount + method + date → an **expense is written and
linked** → the next period's bill is generated by the recurrence engine →
history shows amount-by-month so an unusual bill is visible.

### J6 — Medicine course (V1, safety-sensitive)
Ayesha adds a medicine for Dada: name, dose text, frequency, start/end, qty.
System generates the *dose schedule*. Each day the dashboard shows due doses; one
tap records taken/skipped. Remaining quantity decrements; at the refill threshold
a reminder fires.
**Hard constraint:** the app records and reminds. It never suggests, computes, or
alters a dose. Dose text is stored and displayed verbatim as entered.

### J7 — Assigning work
Ayesha creates a recurring chore, assigns it to Sana (a member with no login),
sets "every Monday". Sana has no account; Ayesha ticks it done each week. Later
Sana gets a login — the same member row gains a `user_id`, and history is intact.

### J8 — The teenager's view
Zara logs in and sees only: her tasks, shared events, the family calendar. No
finance tab, no documents tab, no other members' health data. **Enforced
server-side**; the hidden nav is a convenience, not the control.

## Anti-journeys (things we deliberately make hard or impossible)

- **Bulk data entry sessions.** If the product needs a "set aside an hour to
  enter your data" step, it has failed. Everything is capturable incrementally.
- **Configuring before using.** No setup wizard beyond household + members.
  Categories are seeded with sensible defaults and editable later.
- **Silent notification spam.** Every notification must be dismissible, and
  repeat notifications for the same object are coalesced.
